museum better — more delightful, more inspiring? How can design provide
tools for that?

## Who likes going to museums?

With this many museums scattered across the territory, it would be easy —
and logical — to say Brazilians love museums. That every Brazilian, of
every kind, has a museum to call their own. But the odds are much higher
that you, reading this, haven't visited a museum in the last 12 months than
that you have.

And the odds are also good that, if you did go, you don't have the best
things to say about your experience.

> Data released by Instituto Oi Futuro and Consumoteca (2019), from a
> survey conducted at Museu do Amanhã, shows that almost half of
> respondents don't visit museums regularly. Of those who explained why,
> 52% said they find museums monotonous, while only 20% find them fun.

Another interesting fact about how often Brazilians visit museums: if
you're a frequent museum-goer, you've probably run into a school field trip
during one of your visits. In that same Oi Futuro survey, of the group that
said they don't visit museums, 55% also said their first experience with
one was on a school trip.

Finally, bringing design's voice into the conversation, Don Norman put it
well: if a person's first experience with a service is frustrating or
confusing, their natural tendency is to reject it, avoiding it from then
on.

## Talking about museums is easy — running one is hard

If you're a museum director today, there are already rules, regulations,
and policies governing your work. You have governmental obligations, fiscal
obligations, and much more within the responsibilities of a museological
institution. But how do you find out about all that? How do you learn what
you need to do, what you can and can't do, and what resources you have
access to as a museum?

These are documents that behave very well in closed-door meetings aimed at
structuring a vision for the future, but that fail badly when used in the
day-to-day work of the often multidisciplinary professionals who keep
museums running.

If the question is how to organize existing information in a more
palatable way, ready for quick day-to-day action, we believe design can
help. If you don't believe it, look at what the content design on this very
page did for our research. This site is a practical, evident exercise of
the things we're proposing.

And we also know there are two more levels that make an organization carry
out its role: the tactical level and the operational level.

<PlanningLevels />

There's no documentation or tool today that helps museum workers operate a
quality service, or that puts the recipients of the museum's service at the
center of the initiatives it carries out.

The open questions are: what are the steps to maintain a continuous,
pleasant relationship with local schools, ensuring students have multiple
points of contact with the museum? What tools exist to measure public
satisfaction with the exhibits they visit? And we believe design can help
answer them.

## How this research serves the museum

Our ultimate goal is to consolidate the school-visit journey in museums in
a way that's more palatable and actionable for museum workers. We'll
highlight the touchpoints educators and students have with each part of the
museum, pointing to tools for measuring everyone's satisfaction at each
stage, and ways to improve it.

And this slice isn't small: among Belo Horizonte's museums, educational
activity is already the majority.

Whatever the final format and content of this selection turns out to be, we
hope it's flexible enough to serve every kind of museum within our scope.
There are many — Brazil's museum landscape, like everything about the
country, is diverse.

It's quite a challenge, and what you have in your hands right now is just
the beginning of this conversation. We still have a full semester of

through September, ideation between September and October, prototyping from

## Closing thoughts

We understand this study covers a small slice of museums, in a small slice
of Brazil's territory, resting on just two of the many fields within
design. And that's okay. This is only the first formulation of research
that can branch out across many levels, with many researchers.

And there's still more to be done around museums. We know most museums
have a visitor profile that's not at all representative of our country,
that many people are afraid or embarrassed to walk in, or to leave without
understanding what they saw.

But **you**, museum worker, can contribute to this research right now!

During the second half of 2026 we'll take all this theoretical knowledge
and put it up against field research. We want to understand more about the
